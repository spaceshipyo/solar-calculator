Frequently Asked Questions

How many solar panels are needed for a 5 HP VFD motor?
A 5 HP three-phase motor typically draws around 9.5A. Using 550W panels, a standard solar VFD system requires 1 string of 14–16 panels (approximately 7.7–8.8 kW). The exact count depends on the panel wattage (550W or 725W) and your motor's actual current draw. Use the VFD Calculator above for a precise recommendation.
What is solar ROI and how is it calculated?
Solar ROI (Return on Investment) measures the total financial return from your solar system over its lifetime, expressed as a percentage of the original investment. It is calculated by dividing the 25-year net profit (total savings minus maintenance and system cost) by the system cost. A typical on-grid solar system achieves ROI of 200–400% over 25 years depending on tariff rates and local sunlight.
Can one solar VFD run multiple motors?
A single three-phase solar VFD can run multiple motors of the same type and RPM if they operate one at a time. However, each single-phase motor requires its own dedicated VFD — single-phase loads cannot be shared across one VFD. Use the Multi Motor tab to design systems with multiple motors correctly.
How do I size a solar pump system?
To size a solar pump system: (1) Note the motor HP and current from the nameplate. (2) Choose between single-phase (up to 5 HP / 16A) or three-phase (5–150 HP). (3) Use the VFD Calculator to get the exact panel count and string design. For three-phase systems, the solar array size is typically 1.4× the motor load in kW.
What are the benefits of a solar VFD system?
Solar VFD systems offer multiple advantages: eliminate electricity bills for pump and motor operations, provide soft-start protection to extend motor life, enable variable speed control for efficient water management, reduce carbon footprint, and typically achieve full payback within 3–6 years in most Indian agricultural and industrial settings.
